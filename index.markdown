# UBC Botanical Garden Plant Records Management Manual 

<img src="./figures/campbell.jpeg" alt="the campbell bldg" width="700">

Botanical garden’s plant records are as vital as the plants themselves and serve as a framework for the preservation of their data, the care of the collections, and access and use of the data. Accurate and complete information about Botanic gardens, as living museums, are not only ethically and legally obligated to maintain basic information about their collections according to accepted professional standards, but without proper documentation, these institutions have a limited story to tell and little reference value (American Association of Museums (AAM), 1984, referenced by Hohn, 2008).

In the botanic garden context, documentation, often referred to as plant records, is composed of a suite of policies, databases, maps, and related files that document the institution’s living collections (Michener, 2011). The benefits of well-documented collections are many; most notably, accessible and coherent internal organization and greater potential to contribute to larger-scale efforts by sharing information with researchers, other institutions, and the public (Highland, 2014).

### History of Plant Records at UBC Botanical Garden

>-- 5 *hand written* books listing accessions **1964-0001 to 1995-0477** are stored in the accession tech office. These list taxon, date and source.
>
>-- A specially written, in house program, **BGAS** (Botanical Garden Accession System) was used to manage plant records beginning in **1969**. Innovations produced three versions of the program. The data from BGAS3 was the main source of data for the transfer to **IrisBG in 2015**. Printouts from 1990’s are stored in the accession tech office. Digital copies of BGAS3 data from 2014 are stored. The BGAS3 program may be operable on an old laptop.  
>
>-- In 2010 the Garden used **Bauble**, a free data management program created for the Belize Botanic Garden. This program could record details that BGAS could not, but it was experimental. Records were maintained in BGAS at this time as well. The Bauble program did not turn out to be sufficient for UBCBG needs and the transfer to IRISBG was made in 2014-2015.  
>
>-- The switch to **IrisBG** included a **change of numbering system**. BGAS used a 5 digit incremental number + a source number + the year accessioned. IrisBG uses the year accessioned followed by a 4-digit incremental accession number. The numbering starts with one for each new year. Old accession numbers can be searched in Iris.  
>
>-- The BGAS data was transferred to Iris in 2015. The accessions technician cleaned the data for transfer errors, but the accuracy of data due to factors such as ambiguous dates is reason to question the item histories of accessions prior to May 2015.
